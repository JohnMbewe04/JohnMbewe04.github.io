NeuroClear

Multi-sensor wellness glasses with edge AI

Smart glasses that watch for mental fatigue, poor neck posture, digital eye strain and nearby obstacles. All processing happens on the glasses, and alerts arrive through bone conduction or a companion app.

Role

Overview

Long days at a screen bring three problems together: mental fatigue, forward head posture and digital eye strain. Consumer tools tackle them one at a time, each with its own gadget, and many send data to the cloud. I couldn't find a published system that watches all three from a single pair of glasses.

NeuroClear builds on published wearable-EEG glasses research. Dry electrodes at F7, T7, F8 and T8 read brain activity, a 6-axis IMU tracks neck angle, an IR sensor counts blinks and a multizone time-of-flight sensor watches for obstacles ahead. Everything runs on an ESP32-S3, so no data has to leave the glasses.

Alerts go to a bone-conduction transducer on the frame, the companion app, or both, chosen per alert. With bone conduction and the obstacle sensor together, the glasses also work as an obstacle warning for people with low vision.

What I did

  1. 01Designed three custom PCBs in KiCad. The MCU temple board carries the ESP32-S3 module, IMU, fuel gauge and audio amplifier. The analogue front-end uses an ADS1299-4. The front sensor board carries the VL53L5CX multizone ToF and APDS-9960 blink sensor.
  2. 02Simulated the EEG channel, bias drive and power path before layout, then brought the boards up. With inputs shorted, the noise floor measured 0.13–0.15 µVrms, which matches the ADS1299 datasheet.
  3. 03Built the EEG pipeline in Python, reproducing a published workload classifier: causal filters, blink detection and removal, 17 features per channel, RFECV feature selection and a 200-tree Random Forest. I then ported it to C for the ESP32.
  4. 04Built the Flutter companion app for Android, iOS and web. It has a live 3D view of the head with the glasses and electrodes, a 3D view of the surroundings from the 8×8 ToF zones, history trends, a secured BLE protocol, calibration, research data-collection modes and a built-in simulator.
  5. 05Designed a 3D-printed clip-on, revised from Rev A to Rev C, that mounts the electronics on a standard 140 mm glasses frame.

Signal path

  1. Dry EEG electrodes
  2. ADS1299 AFE
  3. ESP32-S3

Next steps

The project is now in its evaluation stage. I'm measuring classification accuracy, alert reliability, latency and battery life on the glasses, and the results will go into the final report.
